The problem

One feature, three interpretations

A request moves into design and delivery. The reasoning stays scattered across notes, boards and calls, and each role fills the gaps differently.

The mismatch usually surfaces after engineering review or after launch, when it costs the most to fix.

How a session works

Seven steps from request to agreed definition

Steps can be visited in any order. Each section shows whether it is empty, proposed or agreed.

  1. 1

    Add context

    Paste the idea and attach notes or research. Every source stays linked.

  2. 2

    Clarify the problem

    Confirm the target user, current behaviour and intended outcome. Unanswered items stay marked unknown.

  3. 3

    Review evidence

    Every claim is labelled as evidence, assumption, open question or decision.

  4. 4

    Compare scope

    Weigh release options side by side. The chosen boundary is saved with its reason.

  5. 5

    Define learning

    Agree the success measure, guardrails and evaluation approach. Missing baselines stay blank.

  6. 6

    Review together

    Design, growth and engineering each get their own questions on the same definition.

  7. 7

    Share the result

    Approve the definition and export a short summary and a full brief from the same records.

How the AI behaves

It asks and proposes. Your team decides.

Proposals, not edits

Everything the AI suggests stays marked as proposed until someone accepts, edits or rejects it.

Unknown stays unknown

It never fills in a baseline, target or audience size you didn't give it. It leaves the field blank and raises a question.

Every claim is labelled

A statement with no linked source counts as an assumption, never as a fact.

Decisions aren't overwritten

When a source changes, Scopeline flags the decisions that may need another look instead of rewriting them.

What you take away

A summary and a brief that always match

Both are produced from the same agreed records, so the one-page summary for leadership never drifts from the detailed brief engineering builds from.

  • Problem, audience and intended outcome
  • In-scope and out-of-scope items, with the alternatives you rejected
  • Assumption register with owners and tests
  • Success measures, guardrails and missing instrumentation
  • Acceptance criteria and a decision log
