Problem

Returning customers rebuild past baskets item by item before they can order again.

EvidenceSupport tickets mention rebuilding baskets by hand. Source: Support export, Oct
Assumption · Owner: GrowthEasier reordering increases repeat purchases. Test: holdout on eligible cohort

Scope

  • Rebuild a previous basket with current prices
  • Customer reviews the basket before checkout
Decision · PM, EngNo automatic repeat orders in this release. Reason: avoids surprise charges
Open question · DesignWhat happens when an item is unavailable or its price changed?

Why it's different

The same sentence, written two ways

In a typical spec

“Most customers reorder the same basket every month, so one-tap reorder will lift repeat purchases.”

Reads like a fact. Nobody can tell what's known and what's hoped.

In Scopeline

Open question · GrowthDo most customers reorder the same basket? No source linked yet
Assumption · UntestedOne-tap reorder will lift repeat purchases. Needs an owner and a test

Same idea. Now the team knows what to check before committing work.

How a session works

Seven steps from request to agreed definition

  1. 1Add contextPaste the idea, notes and research. Sources stay linked.
  2. 2Clarify the problemTarget user, current behaviour, intended outcome.
  3. 3Review evidenceEvery claim labelled as evidence, assumption, question or decision.
  4. 4Compare scopeOptions side by side. The chosen boundary keeps its reason.
  5. 5Define learningSuccess measure and guardrails. Unknown values stay blank.
  6. 6Review togetherEach role answers its own questions on the same definition.
  7. 7Share the resultA summary and a brief from the same agreed records.

How the AI behaves

It asks and proposes. Your team decides.

Proposals, not edits

Suggestions stay marked as proposed until someone accepts, edits or rejects them.

Unknown stays unknown

It never fills a baseline, target or audience size you didn't give it.

Every claim labelled

A statement with no linked source counts as an assumption, never a fact.

Decisions aren't overwritten

New sources flag the decisions they may affect instead of rewriting them.
